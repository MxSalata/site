On an ordinary day a hospital's integration engine can see hundreds of failed messages. Engineers put right what they
can by hand, and the rest can go unactioned. Shutter won't fix them all, but it helps with most: in my work on
interoperability with 28 NHS hospitals, most failures were of the kinds it knows. It resends the failed patient movements
that pass its checks, closes those the receiver already has, and for the rest stops, writes nothing, and identifies the
patient for a person to review.

The usual fix for a failed HL7 delivery is a resend from the Message Viewer. If the patient has moved since, that resend
can move them back in the downstream system, and it can also repeat something the receiver already holds or revive a cancelled movement. Shutter
turns each failed delivery into a case instead.

**Status:** an open-source contest project, shown on synthetic hospital scenarios. Recovery has been demonstrated
against test receivers; using it with a hospital's own receiver needs integration and validation for that receiver.

## How a case runs

1. **Evidence, sealed.** One immutable snapshot of the message, the receiving system's state and history, and every
   other operation for the encounter. Patient identifiers become per-case tokens before anything reaches a model.
2. **A board of agents proposes.** Three specialists (conformance, encounter, impact) review the evidence in parallel
   on IRIS AI Hub, and a coordinator writes one plan, recording every specialist it overruled and why.
3. **A deterministic gate checks.** Identity, conformance, intent, ordering, state transition, effect, authorisation,
   rehearsal, concurrency, freshness of the evidence, the receiver's capabilities and the catalogue. An unknown blocks a
   write as surely as a failed check.
4. **A person releases it,** bound to the exact plan, or the site's written policy does for the cases it settles.
5. **One conditional write, then verification** in the receiver's own history. "Acknowledged" and "Verified" are never
   the same label.

The agents propose; the gate disposes. They have no write tool.

## What the agents add

The gate doesn't judge a case; it checks a plan. The agents read the whole encounter, the receiver's history and every
other operation, and turn it into a plan with its reasons, or an escalation that tells a person what they need to look
at. In scenario S8 two systems moved the same patient at the same recorded time: the board escalates it to a person,
with nothing written. A recorded run in Rules only, with one gate check lifted on purpose, shows what a plain resend
would do there: write WARD-B over the newer WARD-C. On a real case the full gate holds that resend whoever proposes it,
so the agents' part is the reading and the plan, not the safety net. The project's fair test reports how the board
compares with a single investigator, including where it did no better.

## Pathology results and outages

Orders and results have their own handling. A failed ORU^R01 result becomes a case with the receiver's own response
and findings, and goes to a person: the case itself does not resend. When a receiver is set up for it, Shutter lists an
outage's results that never arrived, holds back any that can't safely be resent, such as one with a newer amendment or
cancellation on the same order, and after one approval resends the rest through the engine, one at a time and in order.

## Try it

Shutter is an entry to InterSystems' "AI Agents in Interoperability Workflows" contest, at release 1.4.4. There are
three ways to see it:

- **The trial** runs in your browser with nothing to install and no sign-in. It plays recorded agent runs, so it shows
  the workflow and the gate rather than new model calls. S2, S3 and S8 on its Scenarios page are a good start.
- **The online demo** is a fictional hospital on real IRIS for Health with AI Hub, whose feeds fail on their own, so new
  cases keep arriving. Sign in as `demo` with the password `shutter` to read cases, ask questions, approve waiting cases
  and run the scenarios from their recordings. The contest's judges have their own account, which calls the agents live
  under a spending cap. The demo goes back to its starting state every hour.
- **The 13-minute video** follows a live run on the online demo, from an escalated case to an approved resend.

To run it yourself, the source on GitHub starts with `docker compose up`, and the package installs on an AI Hub image
with `zpm "install iris-shutter"`. Three articles on the Developer Community cover
[building on AI Hub](https://community.intersystems.com/post/building-shutter-iris-ai-hub-what-worked-what-was-hard-and-what-it-cost),
[testing the recovery gate](https://community.intersystems.com/post/what-failed-when-i-tested-my-hl7-recovery-gate) and
[integrating Shutter at a site](https://community.intersystems.com/post/integrating-shutter-your-organisation-one-namespace-time).
