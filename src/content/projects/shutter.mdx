## The problem

On an ordinary day a hospital's integration engine can see hundreds of failed messages. Engineers put right what they
can by hand, and the rest can go unactioned. Shutter won't fix them all, but it helps with most: in my work on
interoperability with 28 NHS hospitals, most failures were of the kinds it knows. It resends the failed patient movements
that pass its checks, closes those the receiver already has, and for the rest stops, writes nothing, and identifies the
patient for a person to review.

When a delivery fails, the usual fix is Resend, and when two systems move a patient at the same moment, that resend can
write over the bed manager's newer ward. Shutter's IRIS AI Hub agents decide how to put the failure right; a
deterministic gate decides whether anything is written.

<details class="story">
<summary>Why I built it</summary>

These are the failures I met in my interoperability work with 28 NHS hospitals. At one trust we checked every interface
daily for failed messages, and I wrote a 30-page guide to handling them. Not every clinical system checks what it
receives. When a bed transfer from I.PM failed to reach NerveCentre and clinicians corrected the history there by hand,
resending the failures in order could displace several patients, because NerveCentre won't put two patients in one bed
and moves the other to the waiting area. Blindly resending coding messages from NerveCentre to Careflow would overwrite a
patient's coding and end-date it as of today, and on older PAS systems a resent A02 for a past spell could reopen it,
leaving two active spells. That is why Shutter reads the receiver before it writes.

</details>

## A product with a strong core, with or without the AI

I built Shutter as a production-ready product, not a demonstration of a model. Its core logic stands on its own, and
the agents add judgement on top of it:

- **The core calls no model.** It installs on IRIS for Health 2026.2 without AI Hub.
- **Written policy settles the failures it recognises,** with no model, and sends the rest to the agents or a person.
- **A deterministic gate decides every write,** whoever proposes it: a person, the policy or the agents.
- **One conditional write, then verification** in the receiver's own history.
- **A new site starts in shadow mode:** it investigates and records proposals, and writes nothing until recovery is
  enabled. **Stop Shutter recovery** refuses its recovery calls at any time.

What it hasn't had yet is a hospital. Recovery has been shown against test receivers on synthetic scenarios; there is no
clinical safety case and no outcome study, so a site proves its own receivers before enabling recovery.

## How a case runs


1. **Evidence, sealed.** One immutable snapshot of the message, the receiving system's state and history, and every
   other operation for the encounter. Patient identifiers become per-case tokens before anything reaches a model.
2. **A board of agents proposes.** Three specialists (conformance, encounter, impact) review the evidence in parallel
   on IRIS AI Hub, and a coordinator writes one plan, recording every specialist it overruled and why.
3. **A deterministic gate checks.** Identity, conformance, intent, ordering, state transition, effect, authorisation,
   rehearsal, concurrency, freshness of the evidence, the receiver's capabilities and the catalogue. An unknown blocks a
   write as surely as a failed check.
4. **A person releases it,** bound to the exact plan, or the site's written policy does for the cases it settles.
5. **One conditional write, then verification** in the receiver's own history. "Acknowledged" and "Verified" are never
   the same label.

The agents propose; the gate disposes. They have no write tool.

## Where the agents sit

The board is four business operations inside the hospital's Interoperability production, not an external service
attached to its logs: Agent Conformance, Agent Encounter, Agent Impact and Agent Coordinator. Their typed tools read the
sealed evidence and search interface knowledge, and each run ends with a submit tool that returns a finding or a plan;
no tool writes to a receiver. Every tool call is audited in the production's Visual Trace.

## What the agents add

The gate doesn't judge a case; it checks a plan. The agents read the whole encounter, the receiver's history and every
other operation, and turn it into a plan with its reasons, or an escalation that tells a person what they need to look
at. In scenario S8 two systems moved the same patient at the same recorded time: the board escalates it to a person,
with nothing written. A recorded run in Rules only, with one gate check lifted on purpose, shows what a plain resend
would do there: write WARD-B over the newer WARD-C. On a real case the full gate holds that resend whoever proposes it,
so the agents' part is the reading and the plan, not the safety net.

Both agent modes ship. In the release 1.4.3 rerun the board equalled a single investigator at about 3.8 times the cost,
so its measured advantage is not better decisions than one agent: it supplies separate review perspectives, kept
dissent and reasoning a person can review. The single investigator is the economical choice.

## New in 1.5.0

- **A trust's own scenarios.** A site can add entries of its own (a ward, a receiver, a sending system) that send a
  matching case to a person with the trust's instructions. An entry can stop a case and never release one: it has no
  field that could allow a resend or a closure. One person writes it and proves it on stored cases, and a second approves
  the exact version.
- **Set up the site with Ask Shutter,** one receiver at a time.
- **This site's guide:** each trust can print its own.

## Pathology results and outages

Orders and results have their own handling. A failed ORU^R01 result becomes a case with the receiver's own response
and findings, and goes to a person: the case itself does not resend. When a receiver is set up for it, Shutter lists an
outage's results that never arrived, holds back any that can't safely be resent, such as one with a newer amendment or
cancellation on the same order, and after one approval resends the rest through the engine, one at a time and in order.

## Try it

- **The trial** runs in your browser with nothing to install and no sign-in. It plays recorded agent runs, so it shows
  the workflow and the gate rather than new model calls. S2, S3 and S8 on its Scenarios page are a good start.
- **The online demo** is a fictional hospital on real IRIS for Health with AI Hub, whose feeds fail on their own, so new
  cases keep arriving. Sign in as `demo` with the password `shutter` to read cases, ask questions, approve waiting cases
  and run the scenarios from their recordings. The demo goes back to its starting state every hour.
- **The walkthrough** (13 minutes) is recorded live on the online demo and narrated by me, from an escalated case to an
  approved resend; [the two-minute video](https://youtu.be/ZBnAqKTfdvM) is the short version.

To run it yourself, the source on GitHub starts with `docker compose up`, and the package installs on an AI Hub image
with `zpm "install iris-shutter"`. Three articles on the Developer Community cover
[building on AI Hub](https://community.intersystems.com/post/building-shutter-iris-ai-hub-what-worked-what-was-hard-and-what-it-cost),
[testing the recovery gate](https://community.intersystems.com/post/what-failed-when-i-tested-my-hl7-recovery-gate) and
[integrating Shutter at a site](https://community.intersystems.com/post/integrating-shutter-your-organisation-one-namespace-time).

## Limits, plainly

There is no clinical safety case and no hospital outcome study: coverage and operational benefit have not been measured
at a hospital. The receiver remains part of the safety boundary, and an approval is no substitute for validating the
feed. [Every limit](https://github.com/MxSalata/shutter/blob/main/docs/readme/limits.md) is in the repository.
